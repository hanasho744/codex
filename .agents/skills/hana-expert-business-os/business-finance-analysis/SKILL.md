# Business Finance Analysis — Expert Standard

## Use for
Company/account cash-flow analysis, revenue reconstruction, customer/project contribution, recurring expenses, management reporting, profitability analysis, and finance dashboards.

## Core rule
Do not equate bank inflow with revenue or bank outflow with expense. Classify transaction economics first.

## Required classification
For each material cash movement distinguish where evidence allows:
- operating revenue
- customer deposit/prepayment
- supplier payment
- operating expense
- payroll/contractor cost
- tax
- bank/payment fee
- inter-account transfer
- investment/fund subscription/redemption
- owner/shareholder movement
- refund/reversal
- unknown/needs review

## Analysis layers
1. cash-flow by month
2. customer/company/source of inflow
3. project/business-line attribution
4. recurring fixed cost
5. direct project cost
6. gross/contribution economics where data supports
7. receivable/payable/deposit exposure
8. working-capital timing
9. anomalies and unreconciled items

## Evidence protocol
Bank statements prove movement, not automatically business meaning. Use invoice/contract/project context or transaction labels where possible. Preserve unknown classifications rather than forcing them.

## Management views
When useful create:
- monthly inflow/outflow/net cash movement
- operating revenue excluding transfers/investment flows
- customer concentration
- business-line mix
- project contribution
- recurring-cost baseline
- cash runway / obligations only when source data supports it

## Quantitative QA
Reconcile beginning balance + inflows − outflows = ending balance when statement structure permits. Avoid double-counting transfers between linked accounts. Track tax-inclusive vs tax-exclusive basis.

## Output
Management summary → reconstructed cash-flow → revenue/customer analysis → cost structure → project/business-line view → anomalies/unclassified movements → decision implications → reconciliation/evidence appendix.
