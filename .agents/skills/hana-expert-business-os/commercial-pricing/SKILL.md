# Commercial Pricing & Deal Economics — Expert Standard

## Use for
Quotations, landed cost, margin design, scenario pricing, negotiation floor/target, commercial terms, project economics, and customer-facing price proposals.

## Core rule
Separate cost, cash flow, tax, risk, and commercial price. Never use one markup percentage as the whole pricing logic.

## Cost bridge
When applicable calculate:
Purchase / BOM cost
+ tooling/NRE amortization
+ packaging/customization
+ sample/pilot allocation
+ domestic logistics
+ QC/inspection/rework allowance
+ export/customs/forwarder charges
+ international freight/insurance
+ duty/tax handling basis
+ payment/bank/FX cost
+ warranty/after-sales reserve
+ project management/service cost
+ risk buffer
= relevant landed / delivered / project cost basis.

Then model:
- target gross profit and gross margin
- contribution after identifiable project service cost
- cash timing / deposits / working capital
- low/base/high scenarios
- quantity breakpoints
- sensitivity to FX/freight/duty/defect rate when material

## Mandatory context
Record currency, exchange-rate date/basis, tax-inclusive/exclusive, Incoterm, delivery destination, quantity, MOQ, tooling ownership, payment term, warranty scope, quotation validity, and exclusions.

## Price architecture
Distinguish:
- supplier quotation
- verified procurement cost
- internal cost basis
- customer quotation
- one-time NRE/tooling
- recurring unit price
- optional services
- pass-through cost
- contingency

## Negotiation framework
Define internally:
- target price
- acceptable price
- floor price
- give/get items
- non-price levers: MOQ, payment, lead time, warranty, customization, forecast commitment, exclusivity, tooling ownership, freight terms.
Do not expose internal floor/margin in client version.

## Customer proposal
When multiple options exist, compare total commercial impact rather than just unit price. Use option logic such as Basic / Recommended / Advanced only when meaningful and explain trade-offs.

## Quality gates
- Reconcile quantity × unit price to total.
- Verify tax basis and currency.
- Avoid double-counting freight/tax/fees.
- Separate refundable deposits/transfers from cost or revenue.
- Make assumptions explicit.
- Flag unknowns that can materially change quote.

## Output
Executive price recommendation → cost bridge → scenario table → commercial terms → sensitivity → negotiation guardrails (internal) → customer-facing quotation logic → assumptions/open items.
